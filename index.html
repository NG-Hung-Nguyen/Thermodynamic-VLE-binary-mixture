Directions

The diagrams show vapor–liquid equilibrium of methanol and water. The blue curve is the bubble-point (saturated liquid) curve and the green curve is the dew-point (saturated vapor) curve. The shaded lens between them is the two-phase region.

  • Drag the black dot, or click anywhere in the plot to move it there. In the two-phase region a tie line joins the liquid composition x and the vapor composition y at the dot's temperature (T-x-y) or pressure (P-x-y). The bar chart shows the liquid and vapor amounts for 1 mol of mixture.
  • Hold Shift while dragging to keep T constant on the T-x-y diagram, or P constant on the P-x-y diagram. Hold Ctrl (⌘ on Mac) to keep the overall composition z constant. On a touch screen, use the Drag buttons above the plot instead.
  • The slider picks the pressure for the T-x-y diagram and the temperature for the P-x-y diagram. Only the tabulated values are available; the page does not interpolate between series.
  • All series keeps one axis range for every series, so the envelope moves when you change the slider; the other series are drawn in grey. Selected series fits the axes to the current envelope.
  • Click the dot (or Tab to it) and use the arrow keys to move it. Hold Shift for larger steps.
  • Load .xlsx replaces the data with another export in the same layout. See Details for the columns it reads.

Details

No thermodynamic model runs in this page. Every curve and tie line comes from the tabulated data, so the diagrams are only as accurate as the spreadsheet.

Curves

For each series the bubble curve joins the tabulated points (xi, Ti) and the dew curve joins (yi, Ti) with straight segments (P in place of T on the P-x-y diagram).

Phase test

For a dot at overall composition z and temperature T, the bubble temperature Tbub(z) and dew temperature Tdew(z) are interpolated from the two curves. The mixture is liquid if T ≤ Tbub(z), vapor if T ≥ Tdew(z), and two-phase in between. On the P-x-y diagram the inequalities reverse: liquid if P ≥ Pbub(z), vapor if P ≤ Pdew(z).

Tie line

In the two-phase region the page finds the two neighbouring rows whose T (or P) brackets the dot and interpolates x and y linearly between them, so the liquid and vapor ends of the tie line always sit at the same T (or P).

Lever rule

L = (y − z) / (y − x)     V = (z − x) / (y − x) = 1 − L

L and V are moles of liquid and vapor per mole of mixture. On the plot, the green dashed segment from x to the dot has a length proportional to V, and the blue dashed segment from the dot to y has a length proportional to L.

Spreadsheet layout read by Load .xlsx

  • Any sheet whose header row contains VAPOR MOLEFRAC H2O. The row below the header holds the units.
  • T-x-y sheets have TOTAL TEMP and are grouped by PRES. P-x-y sheets have TOTAL PRES and are grouped by TEMP.
  • Liquid composition comes from LIQUID1 MOLEFRAC H2O, or MOLEFRAC H2O if that column is missing. Other columns (K-values, activity coefficients, β) are ignored.
  • Units are shown as they appear in the file. In the original file the T-x-y pressures are in atm and the P-x-y pressures are in bar.

About

The layout and interaction follow the P-x-y and T-x-y Diagrams for VLE simulation by LearnChemE (Department of Chemical and Biological Engineering, University of Colorado Boulder), whose simulations are open source in the LearnChemE GitHub repository. That simulation computes an ideal n-hexane/n-octane mixture with Raoult's law; this page replaces the model with tabulated methanol–water data.

The page is a single self-contained HTML file. The spreadsheet reader (SheetJS) loads only when you choose Load .xlsx.
