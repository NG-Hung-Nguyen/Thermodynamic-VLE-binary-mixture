Directions

The diagrams show vapor–liquid equilibrium of a binary mixture. The blue curve is the bubble-point (saturated liquid) curve and the green curve is the dew-point (saturated vapor) curve. The shaded region between them is the two-phase region.

Load your own binary system

  1. In Aspen Plus, run a Binary analysis (Home tab, Analysis group) of type Txy at the pressure(s) you want, and another of type Pxy at the temperature(s) you want.
  2. Open the results of each analysis, select the whole results table including the header row (PRES or TEMP, MOLEFRAC …, TOTAL TEMP or TOTAL PRES, …) and the units row, and copy it.
  3. Paste the Txy results into one sheet and the Pxy results into a second sheet of the same Excel workbook. Sheet names and the starting cell do not matter. Save the workbook as .xlsx.
  4. Click Load .xlsx below the diagram and choose the file. Component names and units are read from the column headers and the units row. A workbook with only a Txy or only a Pxy sheet also works.

Use the diagram

  • Drag the black dot, or click anywhere in the plot to move it there. In the two-phase region a tie line joins the liquid composition x and the vapor composition y at the dot's temperature (T-x-y) or pressure (P-x-y). The bar chart shows the liquid and vapor amounts for 1 mol of mixture.
  • Hold Shift while dragging to keep T constant on the T-x-y diagram, or P constant on the P-x-y diagram. Hold Ctrl (⌘ on Mac) to keep the overall composition z constant. On a touch screen, use the Drag buttons above the plot instead.
  • The slider picks the pressure for the T-x-y diagram and the temperature for the P-x-y diagram. Only the values in the data are available; the page does not interpolate between them.
  • x-axis component chooses which component's mole fraction is plotted. Units switches temperature between °C and K and pressure between bar, atm and kPa.
  • All series keeps one axis range for every series, so the envelope moves when you change the slider; the other series are drawn in grey. Selected series fits the axes to the current envelope.
  • Click the dot (or Tab to it) and use the arrow keys to move it. Hold Shift for larger steps.

Details

No thermodynamic model runs in this page. Every curve and tie line comes from the tabulated data, so the diagrams are only as accurate as the data.

Curves

For each series the bubble curve joins the tabulated points (xi, Ti) and the dew curve joins (yi, Ti) with straight segments (P in place of T on the P-x-y diagram). x and y are mole fractions of the component on the x-axis.

Phase test

For a dot at overall composition z and temperature T, the bubble temperature Tbub(z) and dew temperature Tdew(z) are interpolated from the two curves. The mixture is liquid if T ≤ Tbub(z), vapor if T ≥ Tdew(z), and two-phase in between. On the P-x-y diagram the inequalities reverse: liquid if P ≥ Pbub(z), vapor if P ≤ Pdew(z).

Tie line

In the two-phase region the page finds the two neighbouring rows whose T (or P) brackets the dot and interpolates x and y linearly between them, so the liquid and vapor ends of the tie line always sit at the same T (or P). With an azeotrope there are two such tie lines at some temperatures (or pressures), one on each side of it; the page uses the one that contains z.

Lever rule

L = (y − z) / (y − x)     V = (z − x) / (y − x) = 1 − L

L and V are moles of liquid and vapor per mole of mixture. On the plot, the green dashed segment from x to the dot has a length proportional to V, and the blue dashed segment from the dot to y has a length proportional to L.

Azeotropes

An azeotrope is where the bubble and dew curves touch inside the diagram (x = y). The page finds it where y − x changes sign between neighbouring rows, marks it with a diamond and lists its composition and temperature (or pressure) in the readout.

About

The layout and interaction follow the P-x-y and T-x-y Diagrams for VLE simulation by LearnChemE (Department of Chemical and Biological Engineering, University of Colorado Boulder), whose simulations are open source in the LearnChemE GitHub repository.

The page is a single self-contained HTML file. The spreadsheet reader (SheetJS) loads only when you choose Load .xlsx.
